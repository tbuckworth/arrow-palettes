Research areas

We work on what we judge to be most impactful. This means staying adaptable in a fast changing world. As the world changes, so do our research priorities.

  • Risk-relevant capability evaluations

    01

    Measuring dangerous capabilities.

    We build evaluations of risk-relevant capabilities, e.g., to measure model’s no-CoT time-horizons, or their ability to autonomously carry-out AI R&D tasks.

    See an example

  • Science of AI motivations

    02

    Building a science of AI drives.

    Why do models seek reward? How does character training influence a model’s motivations? We build tests for models that pursue hidden goals while appearing aligned, and look for the signals that give them away.

    See an example

  • Technical AI governance

    03

    Answering technical questions which feed into policy decisions.

    We try to investigate key technical questions which help improve decision-making.

    See an example

  • Model organisms of misalignment

    04

    Intentionally building models that exhibit controlled, safety-critical failure modes.

    Model organisms can be used to stress-test safety measures and understand future misaligned systems.

    See an example

  • AI control

    05

    Mitigations against capable misaligned agents.

    Work on AI control aims to prevent intentional sabotage by internally deployed AI agents by stress-testing security and monitoring practices.

  • Futurism and threat-modelling

    06

    What will the future be like?

    We try to understand what the future might be like so that we can better predict which research projects will be impactful.
